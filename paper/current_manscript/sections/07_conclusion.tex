\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presents a real-vehicle study of whether today's direct V2X and
network-assisted 5G paths can support compact CV/ITS
messages and correlated CAV operations. The study measures response latency,
deadline completion, and directional goodput as payload, direction, radio
condition, configuration, and shared demand change. To make the two workload
classes comparable, we also contribute IPI, an application-layer protocol that
preserves existing J2735 messages and represents correlated CAV operations
through one application contract, distinct message and operation profiles, and
path-specific bindings.

The measurements establish different operating limits for the two paths. The
commercial PC5 path carried compact J2735 traffic at favorable stationary
points, but larger serialized frames failed first as coverage deteriorated. The
5G path carried larger stateful exchanges, but payload size and network
conditions determined whether vehicle-originated requests completed within the
application deadline. Same-device tests also showed that a
larger nominal uplink allocation did not guarantee lower burst latency or higher
directional goodput, while sustained and concurrent traffic displaced compact
exchanges from their deadlines. Application-ready CAV communication therefore
requires compact representations, stable bidirectional radio operation, and
scheduling that responds to each exchange's direction, duration, and deadline.
